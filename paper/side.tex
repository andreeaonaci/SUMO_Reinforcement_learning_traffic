\section{Introduction}
Reinforcement learning controllers for traffic signals perform well on the network they
were trained on. Our goal is a single shared policy that controls intersections of any
geometry and transfers \emph{zero-shot} to a road network it has never seen. The policy is
a DQN trained with federated learning across several cities: each city trains locally, and
the server averages the weights with FedAvg.

For a long time this policy failed on unseen networks: it gridlocked them. This document
summarises why, how we fixed it, and how the fix was tested. In short, the failure comes
from how actions are indexed in the output layer, not from the learning algorithm, the
aggregation rule, the model capacity or the training budget.

% =============================================================================
\section{The phase indexing problem}
Intersection $i$ has $A_i$ green phases, from 2 to 8 in our networks. A shared $Q$ network
needs a fixed output width, so the standard design pads to $A_{\max}$ and masks actions that
do not exist:
\[
Q_\theta(o_i)=W\,h_\theta(o_i)+b,\qquad
a_i=\arg\max_{k:\,m_i[k]=1} Q_\theta(o_i)[k].
\]
Row $W_k$ is the only parameter that values ``phase $k$'' at every intersection. The phase
order inside a SUMO signal program, however, is chosen by whoever built the network. In the
four RESCO networks we use, phase 1 is a protected left turn in two networks and a through
movement in the other two. Row $W_1$ is trained on contradictory targets and learns nothing
that carries over to a new network. Intuitively, the policy learns ``button 1 helps when
queues are long'', but button 1 does something different at every intersection.

A second candidate explanation is untrained rows: if an unseen network has more phases than
any training city, some rows never receive gradients. On our standard unseen network this
concerns 37.5\% of its actions. The two explanations call for different fixes, so we tested
them separately (Section~\ref{sec:control}).

\section{Method: phase-relational readout}
We replace the indexed output layer with a scorer that rates each candidate phase from a
description of what it does (Figure~\ref{fig:model}):
\[
Q_\theta(o_i)[a]=f_\psi\big([\,h_\theta(o_i)\,\|\,\phi_i(a)\,]\big),
\qquad \phi_i(a)\in\mathbb{R}^{10},
\]
where $f_\psi$ is a three layer MLP shared by all phases. The descriptor $\phi_i(a)$ holds
the phase's pressure (incoming minus outgoing queue), queue, accumulated waiting time,
occupancy and lane count, whether it is currently displayed and for how long, and the
fractions of its green lanes that go straight, left and right. It is derived from SUMO's
controlled links table, so no intersection needs manual configuration.

Consequences: the parameter count does not depend on the number of phases, so no rows can
remain untrained; no index can carry conflicting meanings; the policy applies to any phase
count; and since $\phi_i(a)$ contains per phase pressure, the max pressure rule lies inside
the model class, which was not the case for the indexed head.

\paragraph{Signal timing.} Every 5\,s the controller of each intersection selects which green
phase should be active. Selecting the current phase keeps it green. Selecting another phase
inserts a 3\,s yellow and is accepted only if the current phase has held for at least 8\,s
(the yellow plus a 5\,s minimum green); earlier requests are ignored. The shortest phase is
therefore 10\,s, with 7\,s of green. The 3\,s yellow is the RESCO benchmark's setting. Some of
our internal experiments used the simulator default of 2\,s, which gives one extra second of
green per phase change; those results are compared only with each other, never with
published numbers.

\paragraph{Training and statistics.} Each configuration is trained with six random seeds
(3, 7, 11, 17, 21, 25). A result is called confirmed only at six seeds; three seed results are
called screens.

\subsection{The cause is the indexing, not untrained rows}\label{sec:control}

indexed head is fully trained. If untrained rows were the cause, performance should recover.

\subsection{Comparison with concurrent work}
Braun (2026) proposes a related idea: a graph network scores individual movements, and
the scores are summed into phase scores, so the model does not depend on the number of
phases. We ran the released code, at the version cited in that work, on the same three
training networks and measured it with the same pipeline as our policy
(Table~\ref{tab:braun}).

The method was trained with its own settings: PPO, 85 training iterations, a throughput
reward, a decision every 10\,s and a 20\,s minimum green. This is 85 times the simulated
time used to train our policy. Two versions are compared. The published version builds its
own phases from all conflict free movement combinations, which gives 17 phases on grid4x4
instead of 8; the second version uses the network's own phases. The method's own max
pressure controller on each phase set is included, to separate the effect of the learner
from the effect of the phase set. Every learned version uses six training seeds.

Our policy has lower delay on all three networks ($|\Delta|/\mathrm{SE}$ of 46.1, 11.4 and
12.4 against the published version). Braun's learned policy also has higher delay than its
own max pressure everywhere, so the difference is not caused by the larger phase set. That
phase set helps max pressure on grid4x4, hurts it on cologne3, and leads to gridlock on
ingolstadt7 (44.1\% of trips completed). Braun's method was designed for throughput, on
which it is close to the rule based controllers here (94.5 to 96.9\% of trips completed),
and on cologne3 it completes more trips than our policy.

aggregation rules and clustering, larger architectures, other algorithms (PPO, QR-DQN, CQL,
MAML, evolution strategies), reward shaping, curricula and fine tuning. 32 had no effect or
a negative one, 4 were unclear and 5 helped. Repeating the strongest ones with the new

\subsection{Evaluation pitfalls found along the way}
Six measurement problems each produced a plausible but wrong number: an evaluator that
silently tested on a training city; a training city sharing the test network's file;
untrained output rows; scenario settings differing from RESCO (route files, evaluation hour,
yellow interval); a green phase missing from a network file; and delay computed only over
arrived vehicles. Each was found and corrected, and the results above use the corrected
setup.

\section{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item A control experiment showing that phase indexing, not untrained rows, prevents
      transfer across topologies.
\item A phase descriptor built automatically from the simulator, so the policy needs no per
      intersection tables (unlike FRAP and MPLight) and keeps the network's own signal
      program (unlike Braun).
\item A reinterpretation of 41 earlier interventions, supported by two measured reversals.
\item Six documented evaluation pitfalls, three of which arise from common tooling.
\end{itemize}
Phase based scoring as such (FRAP, AttendLight), zero-shot transfer (TransferLight) and
federated signal control (HFRL) are prior work.

\section{Status and next steps}
The main question of the project is answered: the cause of the transfer failure is
identified by a control experiment, and the fix is confirmed with six seeds on five
configurations, at RESCO's settings, and against FRAP and Braun's method.
